// How The MVP Index Works

The MVP Index

Ridge regression on 26 years of AP MVP vote data — revealing what metrics voters actually reward, stripped of narrative.

Each input is z-scored within its season, then multiplied by its regression-derived weight. The model predicts what should win. The voting consistency analysis reveals where voters deviate — and why.

// MVP Index Formula (simplified)
MVP_Index =
  w₁ × z(WPA total)
  + w₂ × z(Total EPA)
  + w₃ × z(Total TDs)
  + w₄ × z(ANY/A)
  + w₅ × z(EPA / play)
  + w₆ × z(Negative Play Rate) × -1
  + w₇ × z(December EPA)
  + w₈ × z(Pct Team Yards)
  + w₉ × z(Supporting Cast) × -1
  + … 10 more metrics

→ min-max normalized 0 – 100 within season
Weight 1 — Clutch Impact
WPA Total
Win Probability Added across all plays. The strongest single predictor of vote share in our regression — voters reward QBs who win games, not just accumulate stats.
~10.6%
Weight 2 — Volume Value
Total EPA
Cumulative Expected Points Added across all QB plays including designed runs. Rewards durability — voters penalize missed games more than bad performances.
~10.0%
Weight 3 — Scoring
Total TDs
Pass + rush + scramble touchdowns. The most visible narrative stat — voters anchor on round numbers. 40+ TDs creates separation; 50+ is historically dominant.
~10.0%
Weight 4 — Efficiency Bridge
ANY / A
Adjusted Net Yards per Attempt. Trusted by veteran voters who distrust advanced metrics — it bridges traditional and modern evaluation in one number.
~8.5%
Weight 5 — Consistency
Negative Play Rate
Sacks + interceptions + fumbles + TFLs as a % of total plays (inverted). Voters reward mistake erasers — Allen's 2024 turnover improvement was the MVP-defining narrative.
~7.0%
Weight 6 — Roster Context
Supporting Cast
Offensive All-Pros (non-QB) on the team (inverted). Voters discount stats on stacked rosters. Purdy 2023's three All-Pro teammates reduced his candidacy; Allen's zero boosted his.
~3.5%

The Non-QB
Problem

Only 4 non-QBs have won NFL MVP since 1999. What they all share: total touchdown volume that rivaled the best QB of their season, and a team offense that ran through them.

The Non-QB index uses separate regression weights trained only on seasons where skill players received MVP votes — late TDs (21.8%) and total TDs (19.7%) dominate, reflecting the historic threshold required to win.
